# Feature: Table sensors in the web UI (chart and download)

> Owner: server | Last reviewed: 2026-11-25 | Canonical: yes
> Scope: what a user sees of a table sensor's rows in the web UI (#1847, step 5 of epic #1842, ADR-0021 point 14): the chart reduction on the server, and (later parts of the step) the endpoints of the page, the page itself and the download. The sensor kind is `aicontext/features/server/table-sensors/feature.md`; the read of rows is `aicontext/features/storage/table-blocks/feature.md` and `aicontext/features/storage/table-batches/feature.md`, section "The read".

---

## Overview

A table sensor can hold tens of thousands of rows an hour and years of them. A chart of a range does not send those rows to the browser: the server reads the range and reduces it to a minimum and a maximum per bucket of time and column, a few thousand numbers whatever the range holds. A short range (at most twice as many rows as buckets) is answered with its exact rows as well, so a zoom into a few seconds draws the real line.

Part 1 is the reduction in Core; part 2 the web routes of the chart and the download (`TableSensorsController`, `TablePageService`); part 3 the page of a table sensor (`_SensorTableChart.cshtml`).

## The chart reduction (`ITableChartReader.Reduce`)

```csharp
TaskResult<TableChart> Reduce(Guid sensorId, DateTime from, DateTime to, IReadOnlyList<int> columns, int buckets, CancellationToken token = default)
```

Implemented by `TableBlockService` (`src/server/HSMServer.Core/TableSensors/TableBlockService.cs`), registered as `ITableChartReader`. It is a second interface beside `ITableBlockService`, not a member of it, so the test doubles of `ITableBlockService` stay as they are.

- **Range and times**: `[from, to)` in UTC, as `Read` takes them (an `Unspecified` time is UTC, a `Local` one is converted).
- **Buckets**: at most `buckets` (1..`TableChartReducer.MaxBuckets` = 10,000), of equal width `BucketTicks` = the range divided by `buckets` rounded up to a whole tick. Bucket `i` is `[from + i·BucketTicks, from + (i+1)·BucketTicks)`; the last one is cut at `to`, and there are `ceil(range / BucketTicks)` of them (fewer than asked when the range has fewer ticks). The bucket of a row is `(time - from) / BucketTicks` in integer ticks: no product of two times is formed, so the widest `DateTime` range does not overflow, and the last tick of the range lands in the last bucket.
- **Columns**: ordinals of value columns of the sensor's schema (1..count-1; the time is 0 and is not one; a further column of type time, legal in a schema, is not one either and is refused as "not a value column: it is a time column"), each at most once, in the order asked; an empty list answers the row counts only. Per bucket and column: `ValueCount`, the minimum and the maximum (`Int64Min`/`Int64Max` for an int64 column, exact over the whole `long` range; `DoubleMin`/`DoubleMax` for a double column), `NullCount`, `NonFiniteCount`; per column the totals `TotalNulls` and `TotalNonFinite`. A bucket has a minimum and a maximum only when its `ValueCount` is above 0.
- **Cells**: a null cell is counted, never compared, never read as 0. A double that is NaN or infinite is counted in `NonFiniteCount` and not compared, so one infinity cannot flatten the axis. A bucket with rows whose cells are all null has no value (a gap), and its `BucketRows` still counts them.
- **Rows mode**: while the range has shown at most `2 · BucketCount` rows, the rows are also collected exactly as `Read` collects them (`TableRowCollector`: sorted by time, equal times in the order they arrived, nulls kept); `TableChart.Rows` carries them when the answer is complete (no failed block or batch, no stop at the scan limit). Past that the reducer drops them before it would copy or sort the rows of the source that passed the limit, and `Rows` is null.
- **Order does not matter**: a minimum and a maximum do not depend on the order of rows, so the reducer sorts nothing and keeps no rows beyond the rows mode. Rows out of time order, equal times and late blocks are the same to it. Its memory is a few arrays of the bucket count.
- **The scan limit** (`TableSensors.MaxChartScanRows`, default 10,000,000): the read visits its sources (the blocks of an arrival hour not merged, or one merged hour) in the order of their earliest row in the range, as `Read` does, and stops before the next source once it has gone through that many rows of the range. Every row earlier than that source's earliest row has then been seen. The bucket holding that time and every later bucket are emptied, and `CompleteToTicks` is the start of the first emptied bucket: every bucket before it is complete. Null when the whole range was read. The check is between sources, so one hour can take the count past the limit.
- **First and last row**: `FirstRowTicks` and `LastRowTicks`, the times of the earliest and the latest row the answer counts (in `RowCount`), null when it counts none; in both modes, independent of the order of the rows (kept per bucket as a minimum and a maximum of row times, dropped with the reducer when the chart plans again). After a stop at the scan limit they describe the rows counted, those before `CompleteToTicks`, not the whole range: a row scanned in a bucket the stop emptied is not one of them.
- **Incomplete answers**: `BlocksFailed` (stored blocks of hours not merged that could not be read) and `BatchesFailed` (batches of merged hours that could not be read) are counted as in `Read`; `IsIncomplete` is true when either is above 0 or `CompleteToTicks` is set. The rows of `Rows` are the same rows; the counters of `TableChart` are the ones to show.
- **Errors**: an unknown id, a sensor that is not a table sensor, `from` not before `to`, a bucket count out of range, a column that is not a value column or is named twice: an error result (the request is wrong). A store that cannot be used throws `TableBlockStoreException` (the caller answers 5xx). A store that changed under every plan of the read throws `TableReadChangedException` (below; the same request later is answered). A cancelled token throws `OperationCanceledException`, checked before every block and every merged hour; a chart cancelled while it waited for the gate reads nothing of that source (its read checks the token before its first block or merged hour).

### The hour gate: held per source, not for the range

`TableHourGate` keeps the deletion of a merged hour's blocks out of a read in progress. It is a `ReaderWriterLockSlim`, which queues new readers behind a waiting writer: a read that held it for seconds would stall every other table read as soon as the merge's deletion asked for it.

So the chart holds the gate per source. Its plan (which hours from blocks, which from batches, in which order) is taken outside the gate.

**The plan is one moment of the store.** The batch store keeps a generation per product (`ITableBatchStore.GetGeneration`), a number it changes under the product's lock with every change of what a plan can see: every commit of an hour (one whose every row expired or was dropped, which commits no batch class and appears in no list of merged hours, included), every dropped week database, a rebuild of the hours database, a write of moved batches. The plan reads the generation first, then the merged hours, the blocks, whether each hour of a candidate block is committed, and the generation again last. Equal readings: nothing changed while the plan was taken, so an hour it reads from batches was committed before the blocks were listed (its batches hold nothing newer than the listing), an hour it reads from blocks was not committed when it was asked, and an hour found committed is in its list of merged hours. Different readings: the plan is stale and is taken again. Comparing two lists of merged hours, as the plan did before, was not enough (the third review): an hour committed after the list and before the question was skipped as merged and had no batches in the list; a commit without any class changed no list; a commit followed by the drop of its week could give the same list again. `Read` takes the plan in the same way (it holds the gate, so for it only a commit or a drop can come between the steps) and never reads a stale plan: after `MaxChartPlans` (3) stale plans it throws `TableReadChangedException`, which the read route answers `503` with `Retry-After: 1` (`aicontext/features/api/wire-contract/feature.md`, "Table history"). Wherever either reader is about to plan again or to throw that fault, a cancelled token is checked first: a cancelled read or chart throws `OperationCanceledException`, never the store-changed fault.

 Under the gate, before it reads a source of blocks, the chart asks whether that hour has been committed by the merge since the plan. If it has, the plan is stale: the hour's blocks may be gone; its batches may hold rows the plan did not know, earlier ones too, which would make the plan's order of sources (and so `CompleteToTicks`) wrong; and the commit's record may list blocks the merge kept as unreadable. The chart then drops everything it has accumulated and plans again (test seam `TableBlockService.BeforeChartReplan`). The same check runs before the stop decision that names a source, so the scan limit never stops before an hour of blocks merged since the plan. Every answer comes from one plan, whose hours are each read from the source that plan chose:

- an hour still not committed keeps its blocks while the gate is held, since its blocks are deleted only after its commit, and only under the write side of the gate;
- a source decided as batches in the plan is unaffected by deletions (a commit is never taken back except by the retention of a whole week).

After `TableBlockService.MaxChartPlans` (3) plans that were each stale or met a commit, the chart gives up with `TableReadChangedException`: never a partial answer. A commit happens once an hour per product, so a second plan meeting one is rare and a third rarer.

What a chart answers for an hour committed with a block the merge kept unreadable: exactly what a chart planned after the commit answers. It reads the hour from its batches, the rows of the readable blocks; the kept block is not counted in `BlocksFailed`, and the answer is not marked incomplete (the read of a merged hour, in `Read` too, does not look at the record's list of unreadable blocks). Before the merge the same block is counted in `BlocksFailed`. See Known Issues.

Pinned by `TableChartReadTests`: `An_hour_committed_and_deleted_between_the_plan_and_its_read_is_read_from_its_batches` (one more plan, the hour from its batches); `Rows_that_reach_an_hour_after_the_plan_never_let_the_answer_certify_past_them` (the review's sequence: scan limit 1, the plan sees an open hour whose earliest row is at 80; rows at 10 and 0 arrive and both hours commit; the answer counts the row at 0 and certifies only up to the bucket of 10); `An_hour_committed_with_a_block_kept_unreadable_after_the_plan_is_answered_as_a_chart_planned_after_the_commit`; `A_commit_under_each_of_three_plans_ends_in_the_store_changed_fault`; `A_chart_cancelled_while_it_waited_for_the_gate_does_not_read_the_source`; `A_merge_between_the_steps_of_a_plan_never_lets_the_answer_certify_past_a_row` (the second review's sequence, a merge between the two lists or after the listing, with the deletion delay of a day and of 0; each case fails without the stale check); `A_new_plan_after_sources_were_read_counts_their_rows_once`.

`Read` is unchanged: it still holds the gate for its whole range. Its result is a sorted page whose order and cut at a time boundary depend on every source of the range; holding the gate per hour for it was not proven equivalent within this part, and its length is bounded by `MaxReadRows` (measured below: about 170 ms for the default 1,000,000 rows from merged hours). See Known Issues.

### The read walker

`TableBlockService` splits the read into a plan (`Plan`: the candidate blocks per arrival hour, whether each hour is committed, the merged hours of the sensor's period class, sorted by their earliest row) and the visit of one source (`ReadSource`), which hands rows to an `ITableRowSink`: `TableRowCollector` for `Read`, `TableChartReducer` for `Reduce`. Taking one table out of a stored block (with its failure counting and logging) is `TableRowCollector.ReadBlockTable`, shared by both, so a block is decompressed once even when the chart also collects its rows. `Read`'s results are byte for byte what they were: the tests of `TableBlockServiceTests`, `TableMergeJobTests` and `TableReadRouteTests` are unchanged.

### Concurrency: `TableReadGate`

The number of table reads running at once (`TableSensors.MaxConcurrentReads`, default 2) is a Core singleton, `TableReadGate`, shared by the read route of the Sensor API (`TableReadService`) and the table pages of the web UI (part 2). A read beyond them waits for a turn. The route's answers are unchanged (it has no 429: it waits). A host that registers no gate (the Sensor API test host) gets a gate of the route's own.

## The web routes (part 2)

`TableSensorsController` (`[Authorize]`, a cookie user) hands every request to `TablePageService` (`src/server/HSMServer/TablePages/TablePageService.cs`), which holds the logic: access, bounds, the shared read gate, the answers.

Every answer of both routes, a refusal included, carries `Cache-Control: no-store`: it holds a product's rows or says whether a sensor exists, and a shared cache between the browser and the server must never hand it to another user.

### Access

The sensor is looked up by id in the tree (`ITreeValuesCache.GetSensor`), before anything is read. Its root product must be one the user may see: `User.IsProductAvailable(sensor.Root.Id)`, so a Viewer or a Manager of the product, or an admin. **An id the user may not see is answered exactly as an unknown or malformed id**: `404`, `text/plain`, `No table sensor with this id.`, so the routes do not tell an outsider that a sensor exists. A visible sensor that is not a table sensor is a `400` (`This sensor is not a table sensor.`). **No error body names the sensor** (its path or name): between the check and the answer it could have moved to a product the user cannot see. A sensor removed between the check and the first page of a download is answered as an unknown id. No access key takes part, and none appears in any answer.

Antiforgery: neither action takes a token. Both only read, like the other chart reads of the UI (`SensorHistoryController.ChartHistory`, `NodeChartHistory` are POSTs without `[ValidateAntiForgeryToken]`); the app puts `[ValidateAntiForgeryToken]` on the actions that change something (`HomeController`, `AlertTemplatesController`, `ApiTokensAdminController`).

### `POST /TableSensors/Chart`

Body (`TableChartRequest`, JSON, names case-insensitive):

```json
{"id":"6f1c…","from":"2026-11-25T09:00:00Z","to":"2026-11-25T10:00:00Z","columns":[1,2],"buckets":1200}
```

- `columns`: ordinals of value columns; omitted or null: every double column (bid and ask of a quote sensor); empty: the row counts only. Checked by the reducer: a column that is not a value column (a second time column included), or named twice, is a `400` with the reason. The default set never holds a time column.
- `buckets`: the width of the plot in pixels, clamped to 100..2000 (`TablePageService.MinBuckets`, `MaxBuckets`).
- `from` and `to` are required. A body with a value of the wrong type (`"from":"bogus"`) cannot be read at all: `400`, before the id is looked at, the same answer for every id. A body that names no `from` or `to`, or whose binding MVC reports in error, is a `400` (`From and To are required, as times: the range is [From, To).`) **after** the id was resolved and the access check passed, so an outsider still gets the one `404` for every id.
- **Size**: the action takes at most 8 KiB of body (`[RequestSizeLimit(TablePageService.MaxChartBodyBytes)]`; an id, two times, a few ordinals and a number need a few hundred bytes), so MVC never materializes a large `columns` array under the server-wide body limit before the access check; `columns` may name at most 255 ordinals (`TableBlockLimits.FormatMaxColumnsPerTable`, the most columns a schema has), more is a `400`.
- A time without a kind is UTC, a local one is converted.

Answer `200`, `application/json`:

```json
{"mode":"buckets","from":"2026-11-25T09:00:00Z","to":"2026-11-25T10:00:00Z","bucketTicks":30000000,"bucketCount":1200,
 "times":["2026-11-25T09:00:00Z","2026-11-25T09:00:03Z","…"],"bucketRows":[41,38,"…"],"rowCount":43120,"firstTime":"2026-11-25T09:00:00.0412Z","lastTime":"2026-11-25T09:59:59.98Z","completeTo":null,
 "blocksFailed":0,"batchesFailed":0,
 "columns":[{"ordinal":1,"name":"bid","type":"double","approximate":false,"min":[1.08412,null,"…"],"max":[1.0843,null,"…"],
             "values":null,"nullCount":12,"nonFinite":0}]}
```

- **`mode`**: `buckets`, with `times` the start of each bucket and per column `min` and `max` per bucket (`null` for a bucket without a value: no row, or every cell null or non-finite); or `rows` when the range holds at most twice as many rows as buckets and the answer is complete, with `times` the row times and per column `values` per row (`null` for a null cell, and for NaN or an infinity, counted in `nonFinite`), and `bucketRows` null.
- **Times** are UTC `DateTime`s serialized by System.Text.Json: ISO 8601 with a `Z`, fraction digits only as many as needed (`2026-11-25T09:00:00.25Z`), the form `NodeChartHistory` writes for the node Chart tab.
- **int64**: a minimum, a maximum or a value within ±2^53 is a JSON number; beyond it a string with the exact digits, and the column's `approximate` is true (a JavaScript number cannot hold it; the page says the axis is approximate).
- **`firstTime`, `lastTime`**: the times of the first and the last row the answer counts (`TableChart.FirstRowTicks`, `LastRowTicks`), UTC like the other times, `null` when there are no rows; in both modes. After a stop at the scan limit they are of the rows counted, before `completeTo`.
- **Incomplete**: `completeTo` (the answer is complete only before it; null when complete), `blocksFailed`, `batchesFailed`; per column `nullCount` and `nonFinite` for the range.

Statuses: `400` a body that cannot be read, `from` or `to` missing or not bound, more columns than a schema has, `from` not before `to`, a sensor that is not a table sensor, a request the reducer refuses; `404` an unknown, malformed or not visible id (one answer); `503` with `Retry-After: 60` when the block store cannot be read now; `503` with `Retry-After: 1` and `The table store changed during the read (hours were merged meanwhile); try again.` when the read met a merge on every plan (`TableReadChangedException`).

### `GET /TableSensors/Download?id=…&from=…&to=…`

The rows of `[from, to)` as CSV, in the form of the read route's CSV (`TableRowsText.WriteCsvAsync`: RFC 4180, CRLF, the column names once, a null cell empty, times ISO with seven fraction digits). `Content-Disposition: attachment` with the file name `ExportHistory` builds (`{full path with / as _}_from_{from:s}_to{to:s}.csv`, the times UTC), every character unsafe in a file name (`\ / : * ? " < > |` and controls) replaced by `_`.

- **The range**: `from` and `to` are required; a missing one, or one MVC cannot bind as a time (`from=bogus`), is a `400` (`From and To are required, as times: the range is [From, To).`) after the id was resolved and the access check passed (an outsider gets the one `404` for every id). Before, a time that did not bind was read as year 1.
- **Paging**: the rows are read a page at a time (`TableSensors.MaxTextReadRows`, 100,000), from the next page's time (`NextFromTicks`) until there is none: one page in memory, each written as soon as it is read and let go once written, the first page too (the result does not keep it), so a first page that grew to `MaxReadRows` for one large time is not held beside the later ones. A page in which one time has more rows than the page (`RowsAtTimeOmitted`) is read again at `TableSensors.MaxReadRows`, so no row of that time is skipped.
- **Before the first byte** the first page is read, and what it shows is answered with a status and a text the page can show: `500` when blocks or batches of it cannot be read (`The range cannot be downloaded whole: …`), `400` when it holds more than `TableSensors.MaxDownloadRows` rows or one time holds more rows than a read returns, `503` (`Retry-After: 60`) when the store cannot be read now, `503` (`Retry-After: 1`, the chart's text) when the store changed under every plan of the read, `404`/`400` as for the chart.
- **Access per page**: the access check of the first page is made again before each later page is written: the sensor must still exist, still be a table sensor, and its product must still be one the user may see (`User.IsProductAvailable`, on the stored user, whose roles an admin changes in place). A user who lost the product while downloading gets no further page.
- **Past the first byte** the same findings in a later page (failed blocks or batches, the row limit, one time too large, a store fault, a store that changed under every plan, the sensor removed, the access lost) **abort the connection** (`HttpContext.Abort`), logged as a `Warn` with the rows written: the browser shows a failed download, never a file that looks complete without being so.
- **Not one snapshot.** A download is read page by page from a live store, each page from the time where the last one ended. A row that arrives while the download runs with a time behind that cursor is not in the file: a range that is still receiving data (late rows of an open hour included) can miss rows; a range that ended before the download started is complete. Every page is complete in itself (failed blocks or batches abort), but the file is not the state of one moment.
- The row limit is known before the first byte when the range fits in the first page, and when the first page reaches the limit and was cut (a row was left out, so the range holds more): both are a `400`. Otherwise it is checked page by page while writing.

### The read gate and cancellation

Every read takes a turn of the shared `TableReadGate`: the chart for its reduction, the download for each page (so a browser that downloads slowly holds no turn between pages). `HttpContext.RequestAborted` goes down to the read, which stops before its next block or merged hour: a client that goes away frees its turn, and one that goes away while it waits for a turn takes none. Its answer is empty.

## The page (part 3)

The sensor page of a table sensor (`_Sensor.cshtml`, branch `IsTableSensor`) shows the schema (`_SensorTableSchema.cshtml`) and under it the rows (`Views/Home/Sensor/History/_SensorTableChart.cshtml`: markup and an inline script, like the node Chart tab `_NodeChartTabContent.cshtml`; Plotly is the global the layout already loads, no webpack change). The page talks only to `POST /TableSensors/Chart` and `GET /TableSensors/Download`, with the cookie; no access key is in the page or its script.

What it shows:

- **The facts line** (`#tableFacts_<id>`): the row count of the shown range and its first and last row time. The chart answer carries `firstTime` and `lastTime` (exact UTC times, part 2); with them both modes say `N rows, the first at <firstTime>, the last at <lastTime> UTC`, written to the last fraction digit the server sent. An answer without them falls back: in rows mode the first and the last of `times`; in buckets mode bounds to the bucket (`N rows, the first after <start of the first bucket with rows>, the last before <end of the last one> UTC (to a bucket of 9.9 s)`), the lower bound rounded down and the upper rounded up to the millisecond from the full fraction of the answer's times, so the bounds always hold the rows. No bytes per sensor.
- **The retention** (`_CleanupSettings.cshtml`, the "Keep sensor history" row every sensor shows in the collapsed meta info) with two lines under it for a table sensor (`#tableRetentionNote`): the period is counted in whole weeks of rows; rows are not deleted by it yet (#1850, part 4). The second line is its own line in the view: when the deletion is applied, removing it is a one-line change.
- **The chart** (`#tableGraph_<id>`):
  - **Range**: last hour (default), 3 hours, day, week, or custom from/to (`datetime-local`, read as UTC). A preset ends at the moment it is chosen. A zoom on the plot (`plotly_relayout` with an x range) asks the server for the zoomed range and shows **Reset zoom**; Reset zoom, or a double click on the plot (autorange), asks for the selected range again (a preset again ends now). The x axis is fixed to the asked range, so an empty part of it shows as empty.
  - **Requests**: debounced (250 ms: a zoom drag or several checkboxes send one request). A request owns its fetch (an `AbortController`), its timeout and its 503 retries. Asking for anything new drops the current request at once, before the debounce ends: its fetch is aborted, its retries stop, and none of its answers (success, error, 503) is used, so a superseded request can neither draw nor consume the new one's retries. A request without an answer after 120 s is aborted and reported with a Retry link.
  - **Visits**: every render of the panel is a visit, registered by sensor id in `window.tableCharts`. The tree swaps the node panel with jQuery's `html()` and offers no hook for a partial's removal (the node Chart tab and the history have no teardown either), so a visit watches the document with a `MutationObserver` and tears itself down when its container is no longer connected; a new render of the same sensor tears the previous visit down first. Teardown aborts the request and every download pre-check, clears the debounce and retry timers, disconnects the observer, drops the frame's handler and purges the plot (which removes its `plotly_relayout` listener). Every answer is checked against the visit (not disposed, container connected, the request still current), and every element is looked up inside the visit's own container, so an answer of an earlier visit can never draw in a later one.
  - **`buckets`** = the plot's width in pixels less its margins (the server clamps it to 100..2000).
  - **Columns**: a checkbox per int64 or double column of the schema (a further time column is not offered: the chart cannot draw it); double columns are on by default, int64 off; a change asks again for the same range (the zoom is kept). No column chosen: the facts line and "Choose a column to draw."
  - **Names**: a column name is user text. Plotly reads pseudo-HTML in trace names and `%{...}` in hover templates, so names are HTML-escaped into the trace `name` (Plotly shows the entities as the literal characters) and the hover templates are fixed strings that take the name through `%{fullData.name}`: a column named `CPU %{y}` or `<b>x</b>` shows as written in the chooser, the legend and the hover. The y axis has no title; the notes are set as text.
  - **`mode: "buckets"`**: per column a band, a min trace and a max trace with `fill: 'tonexty'`, at the middle of each bucket; a bucket without a value is `null`, a gap (`connectgaps: false`). **`mode: "rows"`**: one line per column through the row values; a null is a gap. Markers are drawn when a trace has at most 300 points (in buckets mode: buckets with a value), so a lone point stays visible.
  - **int64 beyond 2^53** (`approximate`, the values are strings): drawn as `Number(...)`, with a note that the axis of that column is approximate.
- **The notes**: a warning line (`#tableWarning_<id>`, the colour of `nodeChartNote`) when it applies: the rows of N damaged blocks / batches are missing (`blocksFailed`, `batchesFailed`); drawn only up to T UTC, narrow the range (`completeTo`); N values of X were not finite and are not drawn (`nonFinite`); the axis of X is approximate. A muted line (`#tableNulls_<id>`) for columns with nulls: `N rows had no value in X.` (only when not zero). An answer with no rows: "No rows in the range" in place of the plot.
- **Errors**: `404`: "This table sensor no longer exists or is not visible to you." (plot and facts cleared); `400`: the server's text; `503`: "The server is busy, trying again in N s (attempt k of 3)…" with N = `Retry-After` (bounded to 1..120 s), at most 3 more requests, then "The server is still busy: <text>" with a **Retry** link; any other status or a failed connection: the status and text with a Retry link. **An ended session**: the chart fetch does not follow redirects (`redirect: 'manual'`); a redirect (to the sign-in page), a `401`, or a `200` that is not JSON is "Your session has ended." with a **Sign in** link (the sign-in page with a return to `/Home`), never a parse error. The spinner shows only while a request is in flight.
- **Download CSV** (`#tableDownload_<id>`): the rows of the shown range (the zoom if any). The page first fetches the route itself, without following redirects: a redirect (to the sign-in page), a `401`, or a `200` whose `Content-Type` is not `text/csv` is "The download did not start: your session has ended." with a Sign in link; a refusal before the first byte (400 over the row limit or for a reversed range, 500 for damaged data, 503, 404) is shown as its text under the buttons (`#tableDownloadNote_<id>`) and the page stays. On a 200 the page aborts that fetch at once and points a hidden iframe at the same URL, so the browser saves the file itself, streamed to disk. **Why not a blob**: the alternative is holding the whole body in the browser's memory (up to `MaxDownloadRows` rows, about 0.5 GB); the price of the pre-check is that the server reads the first page (at most `MaxTextReadRows` rows) twice. If the second request is refused (the data changed between the two), its text lands in the iframe and is shown the same way. The button is disabled, with the reason in its title, when the chart answer's `rowCount` is above `TableSensors.MaxDownloadRows`, which the view reads from the server config (`data-max-download-rows`). A range without rows downloads the header line only.
- **The feed link** (`#tableFeedLink_<id>`): selects the node `.tables/<the sensor's path>` of the sensor's product in the tree; rendered only when that node exists (the feed sensors are another part of the step).
- **Not shown** for a table sensor (earlier steps; the spec checks the alerts list and the aggregation switch): alerts and TTL (`_MetaInfo.cshtml`), the history, graph and table tabs and the value editor (`_Sensor.cshtml`), aggregation (`_SensorGeneralInfo.cshtml`).

**Time zone**: UTC, as the rest of the UI: the history page builds its dates with `getDateUTC`, the node Chart tab reads its `datetime-local` inputs as UTC and labels its hover "UTC". The page sends ISO times with `Z`, reads the answer's `Z` times, gives Plotly's date axis (which has no time zone) UTC wall times, reads a zoom's range as UTC, and labels the facts, the notes and the hover "UTC".

## Configuration

| Key (`TableSensors`) | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `MaxChartScanRows` | 10,000,000 | The rows of its range a chart reads before it stops before the next hour and answers the part it read in full. Validated 1..1,000,000,000,000 with an error that names the key. It bounds the time of a chart request; its memory does not grow with it. |
| `MaxConcurrentReads` | 2 | Shared by the read route and the web UI (`TableReadGate`). |
| `MaxDownloadRows` | 10,000,000 | The most rows one download writes (about 0.5 GB of CSV for time, bid, ask): refused before the first byte when the first page shows more, cut off past it. Validated 1..1,000,000,000,000. |

## Measured (2026-11-25, `TableChartBenchmark`, Release, Windows, 24 logical CPUs, machine shared with other work)

Synthetic random-walk data (time, bid, ask, no nulls), two runs each; the first call is a warm-up.

| Chart | Rows | Time | Allocated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 merged hours of one table, 1,000 buckets, bid and ask (2,500 batches) | 10,000,000 | 201–246 ms | 710 MB |
| One hour not merged: 360 blocks of 1,000 tables, 167 rows of the table and 7 of every other table in each, 1,000 buckets | 60,120 | 53–74 ms | 28 MB |
| For comparison, `Read` of the same merged range (the default limit) | 1,000,000 | 168–175 ms | |
| For comparison, `Read` of the same unmerged hour | 60,120 | 58–62 ms | |

- The allocation is transient, about 71 bytes per row read (284 KB per batch of 4,096 rows), and it is the decoding of batches, not the reducer: the decoded arrays (24 bytes a row for time, bid, ask), the mantissa arrays of the double columns, and a new zstd `Decompressor` per batch (`TableBatchCodec.Decompress`), whose context is likely most of the rest (not profiled). Dropping the rows of the rows mode before they are copied did not change it (709.9 MB against 710.1 MB: in this range the rows mode ends in the first batch). A decompressor reused per thread would be a small change in the shared codec; it is not made here. The reducer itself keeps a few arrays of the bucket count.
- The times vary between runs on this shared machine: the second set (after the fixes of the review) was 246 ms and 74 ms, with `Read` at 258 ms and 80 ms in the same run.
- With the gate held per source, the longest hold of a chart is one source: about 55 ms for an unmerged hour of this shape, about 2 ms for a merged hour of 100,000 rows.
- The synthetic times are evenly spaced and compress better than real ticks; on the real hour of the initiative a merged hour of one table read in 1.4 ms for about 2,200 rows. Not measured on Linux.

## Known Issues / Limitations

- A download is not one snapshot of the store ("The download" above): rows that arrive behind its cursor while it runs are not in the file.
- The abort of a download past the first byte is tested through a fake `IHttpRequestLifetimeFeature` (`HttpContext.Abort` is seen to be called), not through Kestrel and a reverse proxy: what a browser shows behind Caddy for an aborted chunked response is not verified.

- `Read` holds the hour gate for its whole range (about 170 ms for 1,000,000 merged rows; about 60 ms per unmerged hour of the measured shape, so a read of a range with several unmerged hours holds it for that many times 60 ms). While it does, a deletion of merged blocks waits, and every read that starts after the deletion asked for the gate waits behind it.
- A block the merge kept as unreadable is listed in its hour's record, but a read of the merged hour (`Read` and the chart alike) does not count it: its rows are missing and the answer says `blocksFailed = 0`, so a chart or a CSV of such an hour looks complete (before the merge the same block is counted in `BlocksFailed`). To be fixed in Core outside this step.
- The planner and the batch read use the sensor's current period class and the class range of the committed record: wrong across a change of the sensor's period and for the region of moved batches (#1850 part 4).
- A batch whose index record survives but whose payload is missing is filtered out by the batch store and not counted in `BatchesFailed` (#1850 part 4).
- A week database dropped by retention between two hours of one chart gives a chart with half of that week (#1850 part 4).
- The plan keeps the index entries of its blocks for the length of the read, and a merged hour's batches are read together before the scan limit is checked.
- The scan limit is checked between sources: one source can take the count past it.
